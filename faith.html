Faith & Leadership

My Professional Journey

My journey through BYU–Pathway Worldwide and Ensign College has helped me grow academically, professionally, and personally. As I study Information Technology, I strive to develop the skills needed to become a responsible Systems Administrator who creates secure and reliable technology solutions.

Throughout IT 497, I have come to understand that spiritual development and professional development do not need to be separate. The same qualities that strengthen discipleship, including integrity, service, accountability, humility, perseverance, and continuous learning, can also strengthen professional practice.

Guiding Principles

Faith

Faith provides a foundation for how I approach uncertainty, learning, challenges, and future opportunities. I believe faith should be accompanied by responsible action, disciplined study, and continued effort.

Doctrine and Covenants 88:118 teaches, "Seek learning, even by study and also by faith." This principle is meaningful to me as an Information Technology student because technical development requires both disciplined learning and the willingness to continue when solutions are not immediately obvious.

Service

Service has helped me understand that technical knowledge has greater value when it can benefit other people. Helping classmates, explaining difficult concepts, sharing knowledge, and using technology responsibly are practical ways I can serve others.

Doctrine and Covenants 4:2 teaches the importance of serving with "all your heart, might, mind and strength." I want my developing technical abilities to become another way I can contribute to individuals, organizations, and communities.

Integrity

Integrity is especially important in Information Technology because IT professionals may have access to systems, accounts, networks, databases, and information belonging to other people. Technical ability without integrity can create serious risks.

Proverbs 11:3 teaches that "the integrity of the upright shall guide them." I want integrity to guide my decisions even when no one is directly monitoring my work.

Leadership Reflection

I believe effective leadership requires humility, accountability, service, patience, and a willingness to listen. Leadership is not simply about directing other people; it is also about taking responsibility, understanding the needs of others, and contributing to solutions.

In the technology field, leadership requires ethical decision-making, responsible stewardship of information, clear communication, and a commitment to solutions that benefit people and organizations.

The principles of Think Win/Win and Seek First to Understand, Then to Be Understood have also influenced how I approach collaboration. I want to listen carefully, understand different perspectives, and contribute to solutions that respect the needs of everyone involved.

Personal Agency and Spiritual Growth

During my educational journey, I experienced circumstances that disrupted my ability to study and communicate normally. A prolonged power outage and the loss of my mobile phone created academic, financial, and personal challenges. My phone had been important for accessing course materials, participating in online discussions, communicating with instructors and classmates, and connecting to the internet.

These circumstances required me to make adjustments, seek alternative ways to continue my education, manage additional expenses, and recover from falling behind in my studies. Although I could not control every circumstance, I could control how I responded to those circumstances.

Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28

"Men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will, and bring to pass much righteousness."

This scripture strengthened my understanding of personal agency. Through prayer, faith in Jesus Christ, and responsible action, I continued working toward my educational and professional goals rather than allowing difficult circumstances to determine my future.

Lessons Learned

  • Faith in Jesus Christ provides strength during difficult circumstances.
  • Personal agency means taking responsibility for the choices within my control.
  • Preparation and stewardship are important for protecting important digital information.
  • Integrity and resilience strengthen both spiritual and professional growth.
  • Challenges can become opportunities to learn, grow, and serve others.

Spiritual Principles Applied to Professional Life

  • Faith: Influences how I approach uncertainty, continued learning, and future opportunities.
  • Service: Influences how I use technical knowledge to help classmates, users, organizations, and communities.
  • Integrity: Influences how I handle information, systems, access, responsibilities, and ethical decisions.
  • Accountability: Influences how I respond to mistakes, feedback, deadlines, and professional responsibilities.
  • Humility: Reminds me that technical knowledge does not mean I know everything and that listening is part of learning.
  • Continuous Learning: Reminds me that technology changes constantly and that professional competence requires ongoing development.

Ethical Technology Commitment

As a future IT professional, I am committed to protecting user privacy, maintaining secure systems, documenting important technical decisions, and using technology in ways that promote trust and positive impact.

My study of the Equifax data breach reinforced that cybersecurity responsibility is not only about technical knowledge. It also involves accountability, timely action, communication, monitoring, vulnerability management, and protecting people whose information may be affected.

Future Spiritual Development

My spiritual progression will continue beyond IT 497. I will continue strengthening my relationship with Jesus Christ through prayer, scripture study, reflection, service, and deliberate application of gospel principles.

I want to become more intentional about connecting spiritual principles with the decisions I make in my education and career. My objective is not to separate discipleship from professional life, but to allow the principles I believe in to influence how I work, communicate, lead, solve problems, protect information, and serve others.

Final Spiritual Reflection

Looking back across IT 497, I recognize that my spiritual growth has been connected to my professional development in ways I did not fully understand at the beginning of the course.

I entered the course focused primarily on developing myself as an IT professional. I leave with a broader understanding that becoming a capable professional also requires becoming a responsible person.

Technical knowledge can help me configure a system, troubleshoot a network, develop a database, or solve a technical problem. Character determines how responsibly I use that knowledge.

My goal is therefore to continue becoming a disciple of Jesus Christ while developing as an IT professional. I want my future work to demonstrate faith, service, integrity, accountability, humility, and a commitment to continuous learning.

The spiritual progression represented in this portfolio is not the conclusion of that development. It is a foundation for what comes next.
